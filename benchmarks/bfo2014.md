# Transport boundary scheme: published versus ours

Source: J.-D. Benamou, B. D. Froese, A. M. Oberman, "Numerical solution of the optimal transportation problem using the Monge-Ampere equation", J. Comput. Phys. 260 (2014) 107-126, doi 10.1016/j.jcp.2013.12.015 (Zotero W5FIFMRA, PDF ZGDPYBIE; "the paper" below).
Published numbers: its Tables 1 to 3, transcribed below and checked against rendered images of pp. 118-120.

Problems:

- Table 1 (Section 6.1): T16, the square `(-1/2, 1/2)^2` onto itself with the density `f` built from `q(z)`; exact map `(x1 + 4 q'(x1) q(x2), x2 + 4 q(x1) q'(x2))`.
- Table 2 (Section 6.2): the ellipse `X = M_X B_1` onto `Y = M_Y B_1`, `M_X = diag(0.8, 0.4)`, `M_Y = ((0.6, 0.2), (0.2, 0.8))`, uniform densities, so `f = det M_Y / det M_X = 1.375` on `X`; exact map `M_Y R_theta M_X^-1 x` with `tan theta = trace(M_X^-1 M_Y^-1 J) / trace(M_X^-1 M_Y^-1)`. The matrix is symmetric positive definite and maps the boundary of `X` onto that of `Y` to rounding (`tests/test_second_boundary.py`). The shared specification's T13 has other matrices (`M_Y = ((0.8, 0.2), (0.2, 0.6))`, Caboussat-Peruso 2025), so this problem has no T id and is built in `run_bfo2014.py`, its rotated target as a class with the `SupportFunction` method.
- Table 3 (Section 6.3): the two half-disks `x1 < -0.2, |x + (0.2, 0)| < 0.85` and `x1 > 0.1, |x - (0.1, 0)| < 0.85` onto the disk of radius 0.85, uniform densities (`f = 1`); exact map `x + (0.2, 0)` on the left half, `x - (0.1, 0)` on the right. No T id; built in `run_bfo2014.py`.

Ours: `FDTransport(n)`, the preset `Newton(scheme=SecondBoundary(n), start=Newton(scheme=SecondBoundary(n, interior="monotone"), max_iter=200), max_iter=200)` (`docs/methods/bfo2014.md`): the 9-point stencil, the paper's filter width `sqrt(h) + pi/4`, the face normals of the square target in Table 1 and `N_Y` uniform directions in Tables 2 and 3, residual tolerance `1e-10` relative to `max(1, max f)`, float64, scipy sparse LU.
The sets of Tables 2 and 3 are not ones `volume` and `integrate` know, so there the same scheme, start and iteration run composed (`run_bfo2014.unchecked`), without the preset's mass-balance check; the balance holds by construction.
Grid: the paper's `N_X` (`N` below) with `h = 1/N` on Table 1's unit square (ADR 0001) unless a column says `h = 1/(N - 1)`; for Tables 2 and 3 the square of the grid is not printed (Finding 4), and ours is `[-1, 1]^2` for the ellipse (the frame of Fig. 3) and `[-1.05, 0.95] x [-1, 1]` for the half-disks (the smallest square of side 2 around them), `h = 2/N`.
Error: the max (and for Table 1 the RMS) over the grid nodes of the source domain off the boundary ring of `|grad u_h - grad u|`, with `grad u_h` the paper's centred differences (its Section 3.1), computed as `gradient_fd(result.u, points, step=h)` (the spline through the node values interpolates them) and `gradient_map_error`; for Table 1 also over every node, one-sided on the ring (`ring` columns).
Raw data: `bfo2014.csv`, written on a100server by `run_bfo2014.py`: every row at commit e349bb0 (`just remote-bg bfo2014 env OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1 python benchmarks/run_bfo2014.py --processes 16 --out ...`), then the T16 rows again at 1168986, which adds the `ring` RMS column (`--problems T16`; the values the two commits share are the same).
Every solve is one run in the MLflow experiment `maesolvers-bfo2014-<problem>` (`T16`, `T12`, `ellipse`, `halfdisks`), one thread, CPU Intel Xeon Platinum 8358.

"As printed" rounds our value to the digits the paper prints; a bold value differs from the published one there.
`*` marks a solve whose Newton iteration stopped before its tolerance (`converged` False); its error is that of its last iterate.
The levels follow ADR 0002; orders are least-squares slopes of `log error` against `log N`; a ratio is ours divided by published.

## Finding 1: Table 1's max column has `N` nodes per side

With `h = 1/(N - 1)`, `n = N - 1` cells, our max error prints as the published one at every `N`; with `h = 1/N` it does not at `N = 32` (0.0214 against 0.0220) and 64 (0.0109 against 0.0110).
This is the convention of the same group's Table 7 in Benamou-Froese-Oberman 2010 and of Froese-Oberman 2013 (`sources.md`, `filtered.md`, Finding 1): `N` counts nodes, against ADR 0001's default.
The L2 column does not settle it: with `h = 1/(N - 1)` it prints as published from `N = 64` on but gives 0.0130 at `N = 32`, against 0.0127; with `h = 1/N`, 0.0126 and 0.0063 at `N = 32` and 64.
We do not know over which nodes, or with which weights, the paper sums its L2 norm; the four measures we tried (interior or every node, either `h`) lie within 4% of it at every `N`.

The filter does not matter on T16: the monotone interior alone gives the same errors to 0.4% (`monotone` rows), as the upwind boundary condition, first order, dominates the error of the gradient (the paper, Section 5.2.2).
The error of `u` itself, after subtracting the means (`evaluate`), falls at first order too, from 5.2e-3 at `N = 32` to 3.3e-4 at 512 (`u_max`), where the paper says that first order in the gradient "corresponds to second-order accuracy in the potential function"; it prints no error of `u`.

## Finding 2: on a domain embedded in the square, Newton stalls on the filtered scheme

Tables 2 and 3, and T12, embed the source domain in a square with `f = 0` outside it (the paper's Section 2.3).
From the paper's Poisson start Newton on the filtered scheme makes no progress there (`poisson_start`: errors 0.27 to 0.98, the iterate left far from a root); the full step of the paper diverges from there too (a diagnostic of ours on T12 at `N = 32` to 128: max residual above `1e3` after 300 steps), and from the monotone root on finer grids (T12 and the ellipse at `N = 64` and 128).
From the root of the monotone scheme, which converges at every grid of Tables 2 and 3 (not on T12 at `N = 361` and 512, last section), the preset's damped Newton converges at `N = 32` and stalls on finer grids: the max residual sits at nodes outside the domain, where `f = 0`, at which the argument `(MA_A - MA_M) / eps` of the filter is at its kink `1` (T12 at `N = 64`: `|x| = 1.000` at the stalling node), and no step length decreases it.
This is the stall of `FDFiltered` (#56): the paper's approximate Jacobian and the damped step, which we keep until that issue decides the iteration.
A wider filter width removes it in a diagnostic of ours on the ellipse (`eps = 3` or more converges at `N = 32` to 128), but the converged errors do not approach the published ones either (0.0546, 0.0362, 0.0338 at `N = 32`, 64, 128 with the standard scheme alone, `eps = 1e6`), so the stall is not the whole gap (Finding 3).

## Finding 3: the zero-density part of the square holds the error up

On the ellipse with `eps = 1e6` (the standard 9-point scheme alone, converged at `N = 32` to 128), the largest errors lie at the nodes of `X` next to its boundary, and the median error stops falling (0.0150, 0.0091, 0.0088).
Two effects of the embedding appear in the diagnostics, and we have not separated them:

- Outside `X`, where `f = 0` and the exact Hessian has rank one, the standard scheme's root is not convex at 0.6% to 1% of the nodes (smallest centred eigenvalue down to -1.9 at `N = 128`), the branch problem of `standard_fd.md`, Finding 2, and on the stalled filtered iterate at 15% of them.
  The monotone interior has no such nodes but converges slowly where the rank-one Hessian is not aligned with its two stencil directions: 0.0837 to 0.0622 between `N = 32` and 512 on the ellipse.
- `u(x_0)` in the right-hand side, the paper's device to fix the constant, takes the value that balances the discrete mass: 0.020, 0.014 and 0.008 at `N = 32`, 64, 128 on the ellipse, added to `f` on the whole square, which adds 6%, 4% and 2% of the target's mass, three quarters of it outside `X`.
  Adding it only inside `X` makes it larger (0.10 to 0.21), not smaller, so the discrete equation outside `X` needs it.

The map of the ring onto the boundary of `Y` is within `O(h)` (the image of the ring is 0.017 and 0.011 off `|M_Y^-1 y| = 1` at `N = 64` and 128), so the boundary rows are not the cause.
A smaller square helps while Newton converges: over `[-0.8, 0.8]^2`, the smallest square around the ellipse, the error is 0.0442, 0.0268 and 0.0142 at `N = 32`, 64 and 128 (published 0.0660, 0.0277, 0.0167), the area outside `X` going from 2.98 to 1.55 times that of `X`.

## Finding 4: the paper does not say which square the grid covers

Section 2.3 replaces `X` by "a square (or rectangular domain) `D`" without its size, and the figures show `X` in a frame of `[-1, 1]^2` (Figs. 3 and 4); in Fig. 4 the left half-disk reaches `x1 = -1.05`, past that frame.
`SecondBoundary` needs a square with square cells (`box`); a rectangle such as the ellipse's own bounding box `[-0.8, 0.8] x [-0.4, 0.4]`, which Finding 3 suggests would leave the least zero-density area, needs a grid with another number of cells along each axis, which `BoxGrid` does not have.
Our squares are `[-1, 1]^2` for the ellipse and `[-1.05, 0.95] x [-1, 1]` for the half-disks; the `tight_square` column of Table 2 is the alternative `[-0.8, 0.8]^2`.

## Conventions

- `N` (the paper's `N_X`) is cells per side, `h = 1/N`, on Table 1 unless marked; Finding 1 shows that its max column counts nodes.
- The gradient is the centred difference at the nodes off the ring (the paper's `D_x1`, `D_x2`); the paper does not say where it measures, only "max over x in X".
- Table 1's target is the square, so its boundary condition uses the four face normals, as the paper's ("there is no need to discretise its boundary"); `N_Y` does not apply.
- Our monotone part is `Monotone17`'s operator, continued below zero with the slope `kappa`, not the paper's `max(a, delta) max(b, delta) - min(a, delta) - min(b, delta)` with `delta = dx^2`; the two agree where `u` is convex up to `O(dx^2)`.
- Newton is damped (the max residual must decrease), as every `Newton` in maesolvers; the paper chooses its damping "to ensure that the residual is decreasing" without a norm.

## Not compared

- Table 4 (inverse mapping between Gaussian densities), Table 5 and Table 6 (a Lebesgue source onto weighted Dirac masses, the Pogorelov solutions) and Section 6.5 (the C-shaped source, a figure): their target densities are not uniform, which `Transport(target)` cannot express (ADR 0007), or, for Section 6.5, the paper prints no number.
- Section 6.7 (square onto a rhombus): figure only, and with non-uniform densities in its second half.
